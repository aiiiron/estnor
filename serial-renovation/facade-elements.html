The product

The facade element: a finished wall instead of many separate trades

Each element is a load-bearing timber-frame structure completed to around 90 % in the factory — insulated, with windows, ventilation routing and a finished surface. On site it only has to be set in place and connected.

Element build-up

  • 1Load-bearing timber-frame structure, digitally engineered from the BIM model
  • 2Insulation (mineral wool) within the element, with minimal thermal bridging
  • 3Wind and vapour barrier, factory-bonded
  • 4Energy-efficient PVC windows, fully installed and adjusted
  • 5Ventilation ducts routed through the element as designed
  • 6Cladding panel / facing pre-fitted — surface finished at the factory
Degree of prefabrication

Why 90 % in the factory is the decisive point

Weather-independent

Production in the hall — no work stoppage in rain, frost or wind.

Consistent quality

Controlled conditions, repeatable processes, less rework.

No scaffolding

Elements are set from the outside — no scaffolding, no access to flats.

Fewer people on site

The work moves into the factory — a small installation team is enough.

Renovatable while occupied

Tenants stay in their flats; disruption is limited to days rather than months.

Short construction time

A building with several stairwells in about 3–4 weeks instead of nearly a year.

On-site installation

Nine steps to the new envelope

1 · 3D scan

Capture from the ground and by drone.

2 · BIM model

The point cloud becomes a dimensionally accurate model.

3 · Preparation

Brackets and base rails on the existing structure.

4 · Corner elements

Setting the first elements at the building corners.

5 · Row installation

Element by element, row by row.

6 · Full coverage

The facade is completely covered with elements.

7 · Ventilation

Connecting the ventilation ducts.

8 · Stairwell

Installing the stairwell elements.

9 · Completion

Joints, rainwater system, roof connection.

Logistics

From Estonia — within the EU

Production at the factory in Kiili near Tallinn. As an EU member state there are no customs formalities; the routes across the Baltic Sea and overland are well established. Elements are sorted for installation and delivered just in time.

Quality & certification
  • Quality management to ISO 9001
  • CE marking, European Technical Assessment (EOTA)
  • Ü-mark (declaration of conformity) for use in Germany
  • Member of the Estonian Woodhouse Association (Puitmajaliit)
  • Project-specific documentation to local requirements on request
To be confirmed per project: specific building-authority approvals for the target country (e.g. abZ/aBG, fire classification) should be agreed with EstNor before a project starts.
